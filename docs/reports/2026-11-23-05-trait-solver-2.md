# The trait solver, part 2: coherence, supertraits, dyn coercions, impl selection, auto traits

Date: 2026-11-23

The follow-up to [part 1](2026-11-23-04-trait-solver.md), taking its "what is left" in order. Each step is in the
model first ([formal/lean/](../../formal/lean/README.md), now 12 theorems, `formal/run.sh lean`) and then in
[crates/sema/src/traits.rs](../../crates/sema/src/traits.rs).

## E0119: impls that conflict

`Coherence.lean` adds `conflict_sound`: if some substitutions make two impls' heads equal and the solver proves both
impls' where clauses there, then both apply to one goal, a real conflict. `TraitDb::conflict` follows it:

- first-order unification of the two heads (self type and trait arguments, the second impl's parameters renamed),
  with the occurs check. Heads need not be linear (`impl<T> From<T> for T`). The instantiated heads are then
  compared for equality: the theorem's hypothesis is checked, not trusted;
- the where clauses at the common instance are proved in a *strict* mode. Leaving out what zustc cannot read
  makes `yes` easier, which part 1 relied on for `no`; for a conflict it would invent one. So in strict mode a
  `yes` counts only from impls zustc read completely (`Clause::exact`, and the driver checks from the header's text
  that every where clause is on a parameter: the summaries drop the others without a trace), std's traits only where
  `implements` is exact, and integer literals get no leniency;
- what `Ty` cannot tell apart is "cannot tell": fn pointers with references (`for<'a> fn(&'a u8)` and
  `fn(&'static u8)` are one `Ty`, since lifetimes are erased), and a parameter bound to an unsized type (`?Sized`
  is not in the summaries).

The first run without the strict mode and these two rules had 4 regressions (coherence-fn-inputs and others), and
one test "passed" for a reason that was not its own (traits/trivial-unsized-projection-in-coherence.rs, through a
dropped where clause: it no longer passes, rightly). Both were fixed before the numbers below.

## E0277: an impl's supertraits

`impl<T> Complete for T` with `trait Complete: Partial` and `trait Partial: Copy` needs `T: Partial` and `T: Copy`
(`Obligations.lean`: the impl's parameters made rigid, its where clauses the environment). The driver checks every
impl of a program trait against all of its supertraits, all the way up, with the impl's parameters as placeholders
whose bounds are the whole environment (`holds_rigid`).

## E0277 at a coercion to `dyn Trait<A>`

`to_dyn` checked only the trait's name. It now asks the solver with the trait's arguments: `Struct` to
`dyn Trait<isize>` with only `impl Trait<&str> for Struct` is E0277.

## Impl selection by the trait's argument

`impl Conv<u64> for u8` and `impl Conv<bool> for u8`: the receiver fits both, and zustc called the first. Method
lookup now chooses as the solver's matching of heads does: the impl whose method has the type the call must have,
both through a bound (`fn conv_to<T, S: Conv<T>>`) and in direct and explicit calls (`3u8.conv()`,
`Conv::conv(&5u8)`, `<u8 as Conv<bool>>::conv(&0u8)`). `examples/trait_solver.rs` runs them against rustc's output.

## Auto traits: coinductive

`Coinductive.lean` adds `csolve`, the solver with a goal that comes back round counted as holding, and proves it
against the greatest fixpoint: `csolve_yes_sound` (a `yes` goal is in a consistent set of goals) and
`csolve_no_sound`. The example: `struct List { next: Option<Box<List>> }` is `Send` by `csolve` and not by `solve`.

zustc's `Send` / `Sync` were structural (`Program::auto_trait`) and every type parameter had them. Now
(`Cx::auto_goal`):

- the structural rules are clauses, solved as `csolve` does: a struct by its fields, a type that comes back round
  holds, `&T: Send` needs `T: Sync`, `Arc<T>` needs `T: Send + Sync`, `Mutex<T>: Sync` needs `T: Send`, `Rc` is
  neither, `Cell` / `RefCell` are not `Sync`;
- a type parameter has an auto trait only by its bounds, through the program's traits' supertraits, which the
  summaries leave out for `Send` / `Sync`: the driver reads them from the trait's header (`trait Foo: Send`,
  `where Self: Sync`). A bound zustc does not know (a std trait it has not got the supertraits of) is "cannot tell";
- `is_send(s)` in `fn f<S: Sync>(s: S)` is E0277 (`` `S` cannot be sent between threads safely ``), reported from a
  generic fn nothing calls too, as it holds whatever S is;
- an impl of `trait Foo: Send` needs its self type to be Send.

## What is left

- Associated types: a where clause on one (`where <T as Mirror>::Mirror: Foo`) is still left out of an impl's
  clause, and normalization is not modeled.
- E0119 against std's impls (`impl<S> Into<S> for GenX<S>` conflicts with std's blanket `Into`): std's impls are not
  clauses.
- Lifetimes in impl heads (E0119 of `fn` pointers, higher-ranked bounds) need lifetimes in `Ty`.
- A placeholder's name in a message is zustc's (`__impl0` for `impl Sync`, `a__b__Hidden` for a trait in a module).
