# Region (lifetime) checking, part 37: impl where clauses through the header, closures passed by reference, `mut rest @ ..` (issue #171)

Date: 2026-11-25

Part 36 ([2026-11-25-08](2026-11-25-08-region-check-36.md)) was merged with #182. This step takes what #182's review
left and more of #171:

- an impl method's where clause against the trait's, read through the impl header's trait arguments (E0276);
- the `'static` a trait's signature implies of a type parameter, by its types' structure;
- closures passed by reference where an `Fn` / `FnMut` is wanted (E0525), and a move closure that moves a field of a
  capture out;
- `mut rest @ ..` where the default binding mode is by reference.

## 1. What was added

- **Impl where clauses through the header's trait arguments.**
  - `impl<'a, U, V> Master<'a, U, V> for ()` with `fn foo() where V: 'a` for `trait Master<'a, T, U>`'s `fn foo()
    where T: 'a` is rustc's E0276 at the bound: the trait's `T: 'a` is the impl's `U: 'a`, which says nothing of V
    (compare-method/region-unrelated.rs). So is `where U: 'a` for the trait's `T: 'a` with `Master<'a, U::Item, U>`
    (proj-outlives-region.rs), and an impl's `where 'a: 'b` that nothing gives (region-extra.rs).
  - zustc compared the bounds of the methods' own type parameters only (part 36), so these were accepted.
  - An impl method's where clause is now checked against what holds there: the trait's method's where clause and
    the trait's header bounds with the trait's parameters replaced by the impl's arguments, the impl header's
    bounds, and the outlives they lead to (`'a: 'b, 'b: 'c` gives `'a: 'c`). A type that outlives a lifetime
    gives what its parts outlive (RFC 1214): the trait's `T: 'a` as the impl's `[U]: 'a`, `&'b U: 'a` or `Bar<'b,
    T>: 'a` gives `U: 'a`, `'b: 'a`, `T: 'a` (traits/elaborate-type-region.rs, run-pass); a projection's (`U::Item:
    'a`) gives nothing of U. The impl's `Self` is its self type.
  - What a type of the signatures implies is taken as given, read by the type's structure: `x: &'a T` implies `T:
    'a`, and `&'b &'a u8` implies `'a: 'b`; the parts of a tuple imply nothing of each other (`(&'a u8, &'b u8)`).
    The impl header's types give nothing here: rustc 1.97 rejects `where T: 'a` in `impl<'a, T> Tr<'a> for &'a T`
    and in `impl<'a, T> Tr<'a, &'a T> for ()` with E0276.
  - The trait's method's bounds of its own type parameters are not what holds (`fn run<U>() where U: 'a` says
    nothing of an impl's U). A trait argument left out for a parameter with a default is its default (`Tr<'a>` for
    `trait Tr<'a, T = ()>`). The error is at the bound of the predicate that fails, and an impl written in a fn body
    reports it on the line it is written on.
  - The texts are read past what does not end them: a `;` or bracket in a literal (`[u8; b']' as usize]`), a `:` in
    a default (`T = fn(x: u8)`), `where` and `for` at the start of a line (not the `for<'b>` of a higher-ranked type).
    A projection's inputs are not what its type is made of (`&'a <T as Has>::Item` gives nothing of T), the trait's
    method's own `T` in its types is not the impl header's `T`, and a type that names no parameter and no lifetime
    outlives every lifetime (`where fn(u8): 'a`).
  - What the signatures' types imply of the impl's parameters and lifetimes goes into what holds, so the outlives go
    through it: `x: &'b T` with the header's `'b: 'a` gives `T: 'a`. A method's body is found past the brackets in
    its literals (`[u8; b'[' as usize]`).
- **The `'static` a signature implies.** Part 36's check of an impl method's `T: 'static` was skipped wherever a type
  of the trait's signature named `'static` and T, so `fn f<T>(x: (T, &'static str))` hid the E0276 for `T:
  'static` (#182's last finding). The types are read by their structure now: T behind a `&'static`, or beside
  `'static` among one path's arguments (`W<'static, T>`, whose definition may bound T by it), may imply it; the parts
  of a tuple do not.
- **Closures passed by reference.**
  - `requires_fn(&func)` with `func` a closure that mutates what it captures is rustc's E0525 at the closure: `&F`
    is an `Fn` only where F is (closures/fnmut-shared-reference-requires-fn.rs,
    fnmut-shared-reference-suggestion-issue-118843.rs). zustc checked only closures passed by name. `&mut c` where
    an `FnMut` is wanted needs c to be an `FnMut`, the same way. Where the parameter's type is the reference
    (`_: &F`), the bound is on c's own type: `inspect(&c)` for `fn inspect<F: FnMut()>(_: &F)` is accepted.
  - A move closure that moves a field of what it captures out (`move || { let t = s.1; }`) was "moving a captured
    value out of a closure is not supported". The field is emptied in the closure's environment now, as a whole
    capture moved out is, so dropping the rest frees nothing of it. rustc accepts it.
- **`mut rest @ ..` through a reference.** Before edition 2024, `mut` binds by value where the default binding mode is
  by reference, so `let [mut b @ ..] = &[1u8];` makes b a `[u8; 1]`. zustc bound it as `&[u8]`, and `let _: [u8; 1] =
  b;` was a false "mismatched types" (#182's review). The elements between are copied into an array of their own,
  for an array of `Copy` elements.

## 4. Not covered yet

- **A non-move closure that moves a field of a capture** (`|| { let t = s.1; }`): edition 2021 captures `s.1` by
  value, and the closure is an `FnOnce`. zustc takes the capture by reference, as the field's type, which a move
  depends on, is not known where captures are decided, and reports "moving a captured value out of a closure is not
  supported" at the move. That is a false error where the closure is called once (on main too), and the wrong
  error where an `Fn` is wanted (rustc's E0525 at the closure,
  closures/2229_closure_analysis/diagnostics/closure-origin-tuple-diagnostics.rs).
- **What a struct's definition requires of its arguments**: a path with a lifetime and a type among its arguments
  (`Pair<'a, T>`) may imply `T: 'a`, and is taken to. rustc reads the definition: `struct Pair<'a, T>(&'a (), T)`
  requires nothing of T, so the impl's `where T: 'a` is stricter (E0276 missed).
- **An impl header with a const block argument** (`impl<'a, U> Tr<'a, { b'>' as usize }, U> for W<U>`): the header is
  read to its first `{`, so its methods' where clauses are not compared (E0276 missed).
- **A move closure's other captures after a call that consumes it** (#228): rustc drops them when the call ends,
  zustc at the end of the closure's scope. On main too, for a whole capture moved out; this part makes the same
  hold where a field is moved out.
- From part 36 and before: see #166–#171.
