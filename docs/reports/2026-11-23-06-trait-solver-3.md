# The trait solver, part 3: associated types

Date: 2026-11-23

After [part 2](2026-11-23-05-trait-solver-2.md). zustc normalized `<T as Tr>::Assoc` with `Program::assoc_of`: the
first impl that defines an associated type of that name and whose self type matches leniently. It does not look at
which trait, the trait's arguments, or the impl's where clauses. And the solver left every binding (`Tr<Assoc = X>`)
and every where clause on a projection out of its clauses.

## The model

`Normalize.lean`: an impl carries its definitions (`AImpl`). `NormTo g n u` says an impl applies to `g` (its head
matches and its where clauses hold) and defines `n` as something whose instance is `u`. `normalize` is the
algorithm: the impl whose head matches and whose where clauses the solver proves, and its definition under what
matching found.

- `normalize_sound`: what it returns is a normal form.
- `normTo_functional`: one goal has one normal form when impls whose heads unify are one impl (coherence) and
  definitions name only parameters their heads bind.
- `binding_no_sound`: so a bound `T: Tr<A = x>` whose `A` normalizes to another type does not hold. This is what
  E0271 rests on.

The model now has 15 theorems (`formal/run.sh lean`).

## The implementation

In `traits.rs`:

- an impl's clause has its definitions (`Clause::assocs`, from the impl's member items);
- `Cx::normalize` follows the model: the impls of the trait (or of any program trait defining the name, when no other
  impl does) whose head matches exactly and whose where clauses get a strict `yes`. It gives one answer or none:
  an impl with trait arguments, a clause not read completely, or two answers leave it unknown;
- `Cx::isubst` instantiates an impl's where clauses and normalizes their projections by `normalize`, never by
  `assoc_of`;
- a predicate's bindings (`Pred::eqs`) are checked after its trait (`solve_pred`): another type is `No`.

In the driver:

- the where clauses that the summaries drop (on a projection, `where T::Assoc: Foo`, or on a concrete type,
  `where (): Array<Element = &()>`) are read from the impl header's text and added to its clause
  (`TraitDb::add_where`). The same parse now decides whether an impl was read completely, for E0119 and the
  supertrait checks;
- the bounds a trait puts on its associated types (`type Sibling: Bar2<Ok = Self::Ok>`) are checked against each
  impl's definitions, `Self::Ok` normalized by the solver: E0271 or E0277 at the definition;
- a call's bound with a binding that normalizes to another type is E0271, in rustc's words
  (`type mismatch resolving <W<i32> as Tr>::Assoc == ()`).

## `-> impl Tr`: the hidden type against its bounds

The summaries keep no bound for an opaque return type (a marker type stands for it), so nothing checked that the
type the body gives is `Tr`: `fn test() -> impl Test { () }` with no `impl Test for ()` was accepted. Now, after
`resolve_opaque` has the hidden type, the driver reads the bounds from the fn's signature and asks the solver
(a `no` is a real error by `solve_no_sound`):

- a non-generic fn's `-> impl Tr + Send`: each bound, the program's traits with their bindings (E0271 when one
  normalizes to another type), the auto traits, and std's traits for scalars and plain structs;
- a trait's method declared `-> impl Tr`: each impl's method returns a type that is Tr (`impl Factory for B { fn
  make() -> u8 }` with `fn make() -> impl Shape`);
- a std bound whose arguments are all bindings (`impl Iterator<Item = &u32>`) is asked without them, a weaker goal
  whose `no` stands.

Checking std's traits on `()` showed `Program::implements` does not know std's impls for it (`(): Default` and
`(): Debug` came out no, which regressed two check-pass tests). `()` now answers from std's list (all the derivable
traits, not Display, not an iterator), and the scalars were checked against rustc for each of the 12 traits:
`tests/check.rs::trait_solver_opaque_std_traits_like_rustc` holds rustc's 120 answers.

tests/ui compile-fail: 4533 → 4539 (+6): `impl-trait/projection-mismatch-in-impl-where-clause.rs`,
`impl-trait/in-trait/doesnt-satisfy.rs`, `suggestions/issue-81098.rs`,

## What is left

- `-> impl Tr` of a generic fn (each instance's type), and nested ones (`Option<impl Tr>`), are not checked; nor
  std's traits other than the derivable ones and the auto traits (`impl Termination`).
- Projections by name alone where std also defines the name (`T::Item`), generic associated types, and
  normalization inside type inference (`ast_infer` still uses `assoc_of`).
