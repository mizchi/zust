# Region (lifetime) checking, part 12: shared derefs, closures' escapes and reborrows, annotated patterns, and impls' signatures (issue #1)

Date: 2026-11-22

Part 11 ([2026-11-22-08](2026-11-22-08-region-check-11.md)) was merged with #54. This step takes more of the cases
tests/ui showed accepted, most of them about closures: what a closure's body may return or store of what it captures,
what a call of a closure holds while its arguments are evaluated, and the signature a closure made for a trait object
is checked with. It also compares an impl's method with its trait's declaration by their regions.

## 1. What was added

### Borrow check

- **What a `Deref` with no `DerefMut` reaches is shared.** `*rc = 3`, a field reached through it (`rc.y = 1`), and
  a `&mut self` method called through it (`rc.push(1)` with `Rc<Vec<u8>>`) wrote shared data: they are E0594 /
  E0596 (borrowck/borrowck-borrow-overloaded-deref.rs, -auto-deref.rs). The borrow check follows such a deref as it
  follows a `&`. The prelude's `MutexGuard`, which writes through its deref, is left out.
- **A closure that reborrows a `&mut` it captures borrows `*x`.** A `&mut` capture passed where a `&mut` is expected
  (`|| foo(x)`, `|| s.put(x)`) is a unique borrow of `*x`, as `|| foo(&mut *x)` is. The closure is `FnMut`, so
  calling it through a binding that is not `mut` is E0596 (closures/issue-81700-mut-borrow.rs). A `&mut` the closure
  passes as an argument borrows `*x`, shared for `look(x)` and uniquely for `foo(x)`, and a borrow of `*x` while the
  closure is alive conflicts (E0500 / E0502). Before, the closure held a copy and no loan.
- **A generic fn whose instances all use `Copy` types is checked for moves with placeholders too.**
  `fn dup<T>(x: T) -> (T, T) { (x, x) }` called only as `dup(1u32)` was accepted: the borrow check runs on the
  instance, where x is `Copy`. When every instance gives a `Copy` type for a parameter its bounds do not make
  `Copy`, the body is checked again with placeholders (as an uncalled generic fn is). Only what moving a T does is
  reported there (E0382, E0505, E0507); the instances checked the rest.
- **A closure called holds what it borrows until the call, after its arguments.** `(|x| f(x))(call_rec(f))` and
  `let c = |x| v.len() + x; c(take(v))` were accepted (regions/region-bound-on-closure-outlives-call.rs, E0505).
  The callee was read before the arguments, and what the closure borrows was not held while they were evaluated.
  As rustc's `&c` / `&mut c` of the call, what the callee holds is now kept by a call temporary until the call ends.
- **A closure's captures held by value outlive the body's call.** `move || { b = Some(&a); }` was accepted
  (borrowck/borrowed-data-escapes-closure-148392.rs, rustc's E0521). A capture held by value is a local of the body
  for the borrow checker, and it went away with the body. It lives on in the closure's environment, so at the body's
  end what such a capture holds escapes it, and a loan of the body's own data (another capture held by value, a local)
  is "does not live long enough".
- **A closure whose kind nothing decides that returns a closure mutating what it captures by reference.**
  `|| { || { x.push(()) } }` was accepted (nll/issue-40510-3.rs, closures/nested-closure-escape-borrow.rs): "captured
  variable cannot escape `FnMut` closure body". The function that makes the closure marks it when no bound and no
  expected type decides its kind. In such a body, a returned value that holds a mutable loan of what the closure
  captures by reference is reported. A closure passed where a bound decides its kind
  (`once(|| || w.push(2))` with `FnOnce`) is not.
- **A `move` closure holds what its captures held by value hold.** `move || { vec.clear(); let mut iter = vec.iter();
  move || iter.next() }` was accepted (borrowck/do-not-suggest-adding-move-when-closure-is-already-marked-as-move.rs):
  the inner closure's value held only the loans of what it captures by reference.

### Regions

- **A destructuring `let` with a type annotation binds from a value of the annotation's type.**
  `let (ref y, _z): (&'a u32, u32) = (&22, 44); *y` returned as a `&'static u32` was accepted (nll/issue-55401.rs):
  the pattern bound from the initializer's tree, not the annotation's. The initializer now flows into the
  annotation's type (as for `let _: T = ..`), and the pattern binds from that tree when the types agree.
- **A closure made where a trait object of a closure trait is expected is checked with its signature.**
  `with_int(&mut |y| x = Some(y))` for a `&mut dyn FnMut(&isize)`, and
  `let f: &mut dyn FnMut(&isize) = &mut |y| x = Some(y);`, were accepted (borrowck/regions-escape-unboxed-closure.rs,
  E0521). Only a generic bound gave the closure the region signature its body is checked with. The trait object's
  text is that bound now, its named lifetimes from outside the closure.
- **An impl's method whose regions make it less general than its trait's declaration.**
  `fn foo<'a>(x: &'a i32, y: &'a i32) -> &'a i32` for `fn foo<'a>(x: &i32, y: &'a i32) -> &'a i32`, and
  `fn bar<'a, 'b>(&'a self, _r: &'b mut Re<'a>)` for `fn bar(&self, r: &mut Re)`, were accepted
  (lifetimes/lifetime-mismatch-between-trait-and-impl.rs, trait-impl-mismatch-elided-lifetime-issue-65866.rs):
  "`impl` item signature doesn't match `trait` item signature". The declaration's regions are universal, and the
  impl method's own lifetimes and elided regions are variables. Each of the declaration's parameters flows into the
  impl's, and the impl's result flows into the declaration's (with variance). The region solver then reports a
  region of the declaration that must outlive another it is not known to. Three kinds of signature are not compared:
  those naming the impl's or the trait's lifetimes, those with bounds among lifetimes, and those whose parameters
  have a different number of regions on the two sides (`Self`, a trait's type parameter).

## 4. Review (CodeRabbit, checked against rustc 1.97)

CodeRabbit reviewed #64 three times. Each finding was checked against rustc 1.97. The first round had 8 findings: six held
and are fixed and tested (`part12_review`):

- **A closure's body lost what its parent's lowering told it when the parent was not lowered.** The lowering of a
  function tells the closures it makes that nothing decides their kind (`Insts::kind_free`), and gives some their region
  signature (`Insts::closure_rg`). Both were kept only in the process's `Insts`. A parent taken from the cache, from
  the session's check record, or from a child process of a parallel build was not lowered there, and its closures
  were checked without them: a session's second check accepted `|| { || { x.push(()) } }`. These facts now go with
  the parent's Blob and with an instance's record, and reading them back gives them to `Insts`. The region signature
  was lost the same way before this work.
- **A `Deref` whose target is the type itself** (`impl Deref for S { type Target = S; .. }`, then `|| s.missing`)
  made the borrow check follow derefs forever. It stops at a bound (128, from the second round).
- **A destructuring `let` whose annotation writes `'static`** did not turn the region check on when nothing before it
  had: `let (a, _): (&'static i32, u8) = (&x, 0);` was accepted. It turns the check on, as `let _: &'static T = ..`
  does.
- **An annotation with holes** (`let (x, _): (&'static u8, _) = (r, 0u8);`) was not related to the initializer,
  whose type is not the annotation's. A tuple annotation is now related element by element, and a `_` takes the
  initializer's element.
- **A closure called with an argument that moves it** (`c(drop(c))` for a `move` closure holding a `String`) was
  accepted: the call held only what the closure's captures borrow. It holds a borrow of the closure itself too
  (E0505). `e(e(1))` is still accepted.
- **An impl's input written `&'static`** was left out when the impl's elided result was given its lifetime, and the
  comparison stopped there: `fn f(x: &'static u8) -> &u8` for the trait's `fn f(x: &u8) -> &u8` was accepted. It is
  E0308 now.

Two did not hold:

- `move || { b.push(&a); drop(b); }` is E0597 in rustc too. The type of `b` names a region from outside the closure,
  so a borrow of the closure's own copy of `a` cannot be stored in it, whatever the body does with `b` afterwards.
- `B::go(&rc)` with an `Rc<T>` is E0277 in rustc: a call through a trait's path does not dereference its argument.
  The valid `B::go(&*rc)` is rejected on main too, by the choice between the two traits' methods named `go`, not by
  the new check of writes through a `Deref` (section 5).

The second round, on those fixes, had 2 findings. Both held and are fixed and tested (`part12_review_2`):

- **A write through more than 16 `Deref`s.** The bound on derefs made the borrow check skip the place past 16 of them,
  so `x.field = 1` through 17 `Deref` impls with no `DerefMut` was accepted (E0594 in rustc). Derefs are followed as
  far as rustc's default recursion limit (128) now, past which rustc rejects the program itself (E0055).
- **A call in a destructuring `let`'s initializer** (`let (_x, _): (&'static u8, u8) = (id(r), 0);`) was lowered before
  the annotation's `'static` turned the check on, so the call had no tree, and the program was accepted. The check is
  turned on before the initializer is lowered.

The third round had 2 findings. Both held and are fixed and tested (`part12_review_3`):

- **Past 128 `Deref`s**, the borrow check still dropped the place while lowering followed the chain, so a write
  through 129 shared `Deref`s was accepted. rustc rejects such a program ("reached the recursion limit"). A field
  access whose `Deref` chain does not get to the field within 128 steps (rustc's default recursion limit) is E0055 now.
  That covers a cycle as well as a chain that long, through generic targets (`W<T>: Deref<Target = T>`) too. Checking it showed a cycle through two types (`A` to `B` to `A`) overflowed zustc's stack in type
  inference, on main too. Type inference follows the chain as far, and the cycle is E0055.
- **An explicit `return` or a `?` in a closure** left the body without the escape check of its captures held by
  value, so `move || { b = Some(&a); return; }` was accepted (E0521 in rustc). The check runs at each exit of the
  body.

A follow-up to #54's review, made after it was merged, held too: since #54, blanket impls' bounds were followed only
eight impls deep, so `().go()` through a chain of ten blanket impls was rejected, though `()` had the first trait and
rustc accepts it. A trait met again on the way (a cycle of blanket impls) or a chain past rustc's default recursion
limit (128) now stops instead, and each trait's answer is kept, so a chain of many traits is followed once each
(`blanket_chain_past_eight`).

## 5. Not covered yet

- **Messages.** Some of the cases above are reported with zustc's own words, not rustc's: a `move` closure's escape is
  "does not live long enough" where rustc says E0521, and a closure call whose argument moves a capture it holds
  mutably is "cannot use `v` because it was mutably borrowed" where rustc says E0505. The lines are rustc's.
- **An impl's method against its trait** is compared only when neither names the impl's or the trait's own lifetimes
  (their relation through the impl's header is not kept), and only for parameters with as many regions on both sides:
  `impl HaveRelationship<&ProofReader> for Article` (traits/param-without-lifetime-constraint.rs), where the trait's
  `To` is the header's `&ProofReader`, is not compared.
- **A closure's escape through `FnMut`** is followed for a closure the body returns, and for what captures held by
  value hold at the body's end. A closure passed to a function in the returned value (`.map(|c| .. s ..)` returned
  from a `flat_map` closure: borrowck/issue-95079-missing-move-in-nested-closure.rs) is not.
- **A closure made for a `dyn Fn` whose text names a type parameter** (`dyn Fn(T)`) is not given that signature.
- **A call through a trait's path to a method another trait also gives the type** (`B::go(&*rc)`, where `A::go(&mut
  self)` is implemented too) is rejected as mismatched types: the method is chosen by its name before the trait's
  path decides it. This was the case before this work.
- **`#![recursion_limit]`** is not read: a `Deref` chain, and blanket impls' bounds, are followed as far as rustc's
  default limit (128). A crate that raises the limit and needs a longer chain is rejected.
