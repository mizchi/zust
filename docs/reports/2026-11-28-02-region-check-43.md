# Region (lifetime) checking, part 43: the trait obligations of calls, and the placeholders of their binders (higher-ranked bounds, 1)

Date: 2026-11-28

Part 42 ([2026-11-28-01](2026-11-28-01-region-check-42.md)) was merged with #342. This part begins the higher-ranked
trait bounds (`for<'a> T: Foo<&'a isize>`), of which zustc read nothing: `for<'a>` was dropped by the parser, the
trait solver chooses impls by types alone (`Ty` has no regions), and so a bound held by an impl that holds for some
lifetimes only was accepted. Of the tests/ui tests that write `for<'a>`, 151 of 417 did not pass; 16 of the
compile-fail ones are "implementation of `X` is not general enough".

## 1. The design

`Ty` stays without regions: code generation, monomorphization and the type checks compare types without them, and
types with regions would be more types and more work everywhere. The regions are where the region check has them
already: in the region trees of values, with codes over a type's region slots for what signatures write. This is
what rustc does too: trait selection does not decide lifetimes, it gives region constraints, which the borrow check
solves with placeholders.

So a trait obligation is checked in the region check of the caller:

- **The impl, by types.** A bound of the callee on one of the program's traits selects an impl by types
  (`TraitDb::select`: exactly one impl whose head matches and whose where clauses hold, read in full).
- **Its head's regions are the goal's.** The impl's head is the goal's type, so the regions of the two are equal:
  the self type's (the tree of the call's type argument), the trait's type arguments' (as the bound writes them, with
  the callee's lifetimes at the call) and its lifetime arguments'. The impl's lifetimes are fresh variables of the
  obligation; its header's `'b: 'a` must hold of them. `Summary::impl_head` keeps the codes of the head (its self
  type, the trait's arguments, the declared outlives), where it names a lifetime.
- **The binder's lifetimes are placeholders.** A lifetime the bound names that is not the callee's is the bound's own
  (`for<'a>`): a placeholder of the call, a universal region that only itself and `'static` outlive. A region of the
  impl's that must be it and also `'static`, a lifetime of the caller or another placeholder makes two universal
  regions outlive each other, which the check reports, here as rustc's "implementation of `Foo` is not general
  enough".
- **Nested obligations.** The impl's where clauses on such traits are obligations of the impl's lifetimes in turn
  (`impl<T> DeserializeOwned for T where T: for<'de> Deserialize<'de>`).

The obligations are made only for bounds on traits some impl of which has regions to relate
(`TraitDb::lt_traits`: an impl head that names a lifetime, `'static` too, or binds one in a fn pointer type, or
declares an outlives, and the traits whose impls have where clauses of those). A program without them does none of
this; elsewhere, a call looks its callee's bounds up in that set.

## 2. What was added

- **"implementation of `Foo` is not general enough".**
  - `T: for<'a> Foo<&'a isize>` with `impl Foo<&'static isize> for StaticInt`, or with `impl<'a> Foo<&'a isize> for &'a u32` and
    `T = &'a u32` (the caller's `'a`).
  - `T: for<'a, 'b> Foo<(&'a isize, &'b isize)>` with `impl<'a> Foo<(&'a isize, &'a isize)> for SomeStruct`.
  - `T: for<'q> Bar<'q>` with `impl Bar<'static> for u16`.
  - `T: DeserializeOwned` through `impl<T> DeserializeOwned for T where T: for<'de> Deserialize<'de>` and `impl<'de: 'a, 'a>
    Deserialize<'de> for &'a str` with `T = &'static str`.
- **The same relations without a binder.** `T: Foo<&'a isize>` of a callee's `'a` with `impl Foo<&'static isize> for St`
  makes `'a` 'static at the call: `want_a::<St>(&x)` of a local `x` is E0597, as in rustc.
- **The region check of a function whose signature has no regions** is turned on at a call with such obligations
  (`fn give_static() { want_hrtb::<StaticInt>() }`).
- **A bound's tuple arguments** (`Foo<(&'a isize, &'b isize)>`), which the solver's reading takes for `Fn(..)`'s, are read
  for the obligation.

## 5. Not covered yet

The next parts of the higher-ranked bounds:

- **Regions of the outer universe equal to a placeholder.** A region the goal had before the call (`want_hrtb::<&u32>()`,
  whose elided lifetime is an inference variable) may not be a placeholder: rustc's universes. Here it may be, so
  that is accepted (rustc's "not general enough").
- **A placeholder that must outlive another region** (`impl<'a, 'b: 'a> Foo<'b> for FooS<'a>` for `F: for<'b> Foo<'b>`,
  higher-ranked/trait-bounds/due-to-where-clause.rs): a placeholder may contain no point of the body (rustc's
  `check_bound_universal_region`). Accepted here.
- **Fn pointer types' binders.** `impl<'a> NotHR for fn(&'a ())` and a `fn(&())` (higher-ranked), or `impl<A> Foo for
  fn(A)` and `fn(&i32)` (hr-fn-ptr-trait-impl-mismatch-29061.rs, hrtb-fn-ptr-impl-not-general-enough-57936.rs,
  issue-46989.rs): the trees do not tell a region a fn pointer type binds from one it does not, nor the binders nested
  in a type (hrtb-exists-forall-trait-*.rs).
- **Subtyping of higher-ranked types** ("one type is more general than the other", E0308), and closures whose bound is
  higher-ranked (hrtb-perfect-forwarding.rs).
- **Where clauses on other than a type parameter** (`for<'a> &'a T: Bar`), **a placeholder's environment**
  (`for<'tcx> F: Foo<'tcx>` of a where clause `F: Foo<'x>`, hrtb-higher-ranker-supertraits.rs), and **normalization
  under a binder** (`for<'a> <T as Tr<'a>>::Assoc`).
- From part 42 and before: see #166–#171.
